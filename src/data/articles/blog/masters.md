# Master's Application Process

Have you seen those LinkedIn posts where people share their success stories about getting into their dream universities abroad? They write:

> "I'm so excited to announce that I've been accepted into the Master's program at XYZ University!"

> "So thrilled to be starting my Master's journey at ABC University!"

But they never talk about the process they went through to get there. From the first application to the moment you land in the new country, the amount of work and money that goes into the process is simply overlooked.

I'm here to tell you about my journey of applying to Master's programs abroad. I hope this will give you a better understanding of what it takes to get into any university abroad. There's probably hundreds of articles out there that talk about what qualifies you but I'm here to tell you about the process itself.

Since I chose to apply to universities in the United States, I will be talking about the process of applying to US universities. The process might be different for other countries but I'm sure there are some similarities. I'll also have a investment counter at each step to give you an idea of how much I had to spend.

## Qualifying Exams

Most universities in the US require you to take a standardized tests like GRE and TOEFL. These exams are conducted by ETS and most of the original materials costs a lot. Due to personal reasons, I had to postpone my GRE exam once so just for GRE, I spent $220 + $50 (rescheduling fee) = $270.

For TOEFL, It's $200.

Anything that is expensive has a free version somewhere. You just have to look for it. I found a GitHub repo called <a href="https://github.com/rishiloyola/Awesome-GRE-Materials" target="_blank" class="FancyLink" data-type="Bracket">Awesome GRE Materials</a> with all the free resources.

Similarly, there are a lot of free resources for TOEFL on GitHub as well. Just search for it.

<b>
Investment: $470
</b>

## Shortlisting Universities

I spent at least 2 months shortlisting universities. Every application costs roughly ~$80 so you can't just apply to every university you find. You have to be very selective. I shortlisted 22 universities and applied to 7 of them.

## Statement of Purpose

The bread and butter of your application. This is where you tell the university why you want to study there and why you're the best fit for the program. Ironically, I spent very less time on this than I should've. I wrote 2 drafts and used the second draft as a template for all the universities I applied to while just changing the name of the university and the program.

It was so good I had a couple of people asking me if I used ChatGPT to write it. I didn't. Used some AI checker to check for grammar and plagiarism.

I got a SoP from a senior who got into a good university and god bless him it was my starting point when I didn't know how to start. I'll pay it forward by sharing my SoP drafts here.

## Letters of Recommendation

If SoP is the bread and butter, LoRs are the plate and fork. Without this base, your SoP is just you boasting about yourself. I got my LoRs from my professors and my manager.

I had to do 2 on-campus jobs in with 2 professors in the same semester to get the LoRs. I would say this semester was the most stressful semester I've ever had. I had to do 2 jobs a research project and a freelance project. Since everything was programming based, I was somehow able to pull through. Knowing how mentally exhausting it was, I don't know if I would do it again.

- First on-campus job was in Nano-Technology Computational Lab(NCL) where I had to migrate the lab’s Network File System (NFS) from the university’s data center to a local machine in the lab. This was to reduce the cost of using the university’s data center. All the machines were using CentOS 7 and all the forums and tutorials were only half working. I spent many nights breaking my head over why a certain command wasn't working. I had to learn a lot about NFS, CentOS, and Linux in general. I also had to write a report on the migration process and present it to the lab members. I was able to complete the project in 2 months and the professor was very happy with my work gave me a letter of recommendation. <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1DR7nv6qnY2-lX0DQZaXm6NSCWBcOzgXcw0bieYWL13s/edit?usp=sharing" target="_blank" class="FancyLink" data-type="Bracket">Here's the draft for the LoR</a>

- Second on-campus job was Building a website for the new Maker Space in the university. I had to build a website that would allow students to book the equipment in the Maker Space. I can honestly write a separate article on this project. Although the project was not complete, I was able to deliver all the features we had discussed in the beginning. The professor was very happy with my work and gave me a letter of recommendation. <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1V3GF_vV_5Yw1CALn99C98wNEb7M1AhMtAvtj2dvpjTo/edit?usp=sharing" target="_blank" class="FancyLink" data-type="Bracket">Here's the draft for the LoR</a>

- Only a few universities required a third LoR. I got this from my manager/friend who I worked with on a couple of freelance projects. <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ymYv-_kwLUXzLHyPGLUT5qPa5OKD52J6pVN5u13NJOA/edit?usp=sharing" target="_blank" class="FancyLink" data-type="Bracket">Here's the draft for the LoR</a>

You should have at least 3-4 professors who know you well enough to write a good LoR. Sometimes, you are not the only one asking for a LoR from a professor so they might just ignore you after submitting a couple of LoRs. So, have a backup plan.

## Waiting

The most stressful part of the process. You have to wait for the university to get back to you. Some universities take 2 weeks, some take 2 months. I got my first admit from Penn State by the end of January. I was happy I got into at least one university. CMU was the last university to get back to me.

## Visa Process

Now that you have an admit, you have to apply for a visa. And boy you have to splurge on this.

- Visa Fee: $160
- SEVIS Fee: $350

Once you pay these fees, you have to schedule an appointment. If you thought that hard part was over, you're wrong. For 3 weeks, I had my laptop open next to my bed and I would check if there were any slots available. Believing US consulate's video on Twitter, I thought the slots would open on 20th May but they released a bulk of slots few days earlier, just on the day I went out with my friends after a very very very long time. Thankfully, I was able to get a slot when I came back and checked at 3 AM.
Thinking about how traumatizing the slot booking process was just makes me shiver.

### Visa Interview

Biometrics was simple. Just go to the VFS center, give your biometrics and come back. The interview was the most anxious part of the process. Thankfully I was able to prepare for it. There's a telegram group called "US Visa Interview Experiences" where people share their experiences and you can learn from them.

I wrote a little script to download the chat from telegram group, parse it and extract all the questions, answers and whether the visa was approved or not. I read over 4,000 responses and I made a list of commonly asked questions for my major and similar majors.

### Flight Tickets

Don't wait for the visa to book your flight tickets. The earlier you book, the cheaper it is. In the unfortunate event that your visa is rejected, you can still get a 80% refund on your tickets.

If you have a bit more money to spare, do yourself a favor and pay for extra baggage. You can't pack your whole life in 2 bags(unless you're me).

<b>
Investment: $1560 + $1132 = $2692
</b>

## Accommodation

If your university doesn't provide on-campus housing, you have to find an apartment. Forget about getting a single room if you're on a money saving spree.

- Find seniors on LinkedIn and know where most of the students live.
- Get a couple of people together and rent a 2BHK or 3BHK.

I was able to find 4 people and we rented a 3BHK. I chose to stay in the dining room because I could talk down my share of the rent to just $230 per month. No kidding, there's no way anyone could live this cheap in this area so I'm happy to give up the privacy for this.

The total rent is $2175 per month and a security deposit of $4350. An application fee of $75 was charged too.

<b>
Investment: $2692 + $75 = $2767
</b>

## Loan

After all the processes, I thought taking a loan would be the easiest part. Boy was I wrong. Bank people are just...ugh painful. I had to visit the bank at least 20 times to get the loan approved. I had to pay a lot of fees to get the loan sanctioned.

- Legal fees: $100
- Property Valuation: $100
- Mortgage fees: $680
- Property Insurance: $60
- Life Insurance: $1098

It felt like I had to take a loan to get the loan.

<b>
Investment: $2767 + $100 + $100 + $680 + $60 + $1098 = $4805
</b>

Fortunately, I got a scholarship of $12,000 from the university so It's not all bad.

## Conclusion

A rough total of $4805(Rs.402880) was spent in the process, from the first exam to the moment I landed in the US.

I hope this article gave you a better understanding of what goes behind the scenes when someone says they got into a university abroad.

I was fortunate enough to have a good support system to help me financially and emotionally through this process. I hope you have the same.
